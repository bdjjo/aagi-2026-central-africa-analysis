# Executive summary and headline statistics

## One-paragraph executive summary

The 2026 Africa AI Governance Index (AAGI) places all six Central African
countries it covers — Cameroon, Gabon, the Democratic Republic of Congo, the
Republic of Congo, Chad, and the Central African Republic — below its
*Established* governance threshold of 3.0 on a 0–4 scale, with reported
composites ranging from roughly 1.5 for Gabon, the DRC, and the Republic of
Congo down to 0.2 for the Central African Republic (Report p. 2). The decisive
regional pattern is not a uniform absence of policy but a gap between intent and
delivery: *Strategy and Vision* is the strongest average pillar (~1.53/4) while
*Implementation and Impact* is the weakest (~0.57/4), indicating that national
strategies, legal foundations, and regional commitments are advancing faster
than the budgets, institutions, skills, infrastructure, and monitoring systems
needed to turn them into measurable outcomes (Report p. 2; interpretation). The
six countries follow different pathways rather than one — strategy-led in
Cameroon and the DRC, governance- and integration-led in Gabon and the Republic
of Congo, capacity-constrained in Chad, and evidence-scarce in the CAR — so a
single ranking conceals more than it reveals. A structural caveat frames the
whole exercise: the supplied data-collection workbook contains only eight
populated indicator rows out of eighty, so the published results cannot yet be
reproduced from source records, and the index's own rule that a zero denotes *no
findable evidence* rather than *proven absence* must travel with every score.
The practical priority for the region is therefore an implementation layer —
accountable institutions, disbursed budgets, redress channels, shared evaluation
capacity, and published monitoring — supported by regional cooperation rather
than by additional high-level strategies.

*Scope note: these figures describe the six countries in the report's page-2
summary. A broader chapter build covering additional Central African states is a
separate dataset with different composites and must not be compared cell-for-cell
with these numbers.*

## Five pull-quote headline statistics

1. **0 of 6.** No Central African country in the AAGI 2026 sample reaches the
   index's *Established* governance threshold of 3.0; composites run from about
   1.5 to 0.2. *(Report p. 2.)*

2. **1.53 vs 0.57.** *Strategy and Vision* is the region's strongest pillar on
   average; *Implementation and Impact* is the weakest — the clearest signal that
   the gap is in delivery, not in ambition. *(Report p. 2; regional means, n = 6.)*

3. **A 1.3-point strategy-to-implementation gap.** Cameroon and Chad each show
   the widest internal split — high strategy scores (2.1 and 1.4) sitting above
   near-absent implementation (0.8 and 0.1). *(Report p. 2.)*

4. **8 of 80.** The supplied data-collection workbook contains just eight
   populated indicator rows out of eighty, so the six-country results cannot yet
   be reproduced from source records — a transparency gap, not a verdict on the
   findings. *(Workbook P1–P8; audit.)*

5. **Same score, different countries.** Gabon, the DRC, and the Republic of
   Congo all sit at a composite of ~1.5, yet their strengths diverge — Gabon on
   governance and integration, the DRC on strategy and innovation, the Republic
   of Congo on governance and human capital — so profiles, not rankings, should
   guide policy. *(Report p. 2; interpretation.)*
